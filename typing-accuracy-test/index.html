Typing Accuracy Test

Speed means little if every third word needs fixing. This test measures the error rate your fingers actually produce — with scoring that corrections can't game.

How accuracy is scored here

Most typing tests quietly forgive corrections: backspace over a mistake, retype it, and the error disappears from your percentage. That produces flattering numbers and hides the habits slowing you down.

TypeSprint uses first-strike scoring: every character position in the passage is scored exactly once, on the first keystroke that reaches it. What each input event does to your counters:

EventCharactersCorrectErrors
First keystroke at a new position, matches the text+1+1—
First keystroke at a new position, wrong character+1—+1
Typing past the end of the passage+1—+1
Backspace / deletion———
Retyping an already-scored position———

Accuracy is simply correct ÷ scored positions. Corrections cost you the error you made; they can't retroactively erase it — and a type-delete-retype loop adds nothing, so the percentage can't be farmed.

Why accuracy beats raw speed

Every error costs you at least three keystrokes: the wrong character, the backspace, and the retype — plus the moment it takes to notice. A 70 WPM typist at 90% accuracy usually finishes real work slower than a 60 WPM typist at 98%.

That's why the test reports Net WPM (correct characters only) next to Raw WPM (everything typed). A large gap between them is an accuracy problem wearing a speed costume. The typing speed test page explains all four reported metrics.

How to practice accuracy deliberately

Three things in the app are built for exactly this:

  • Slow down one gear. Run the 60-second test at a pace where you stay above 95%, and only speed up when the percentage holds.
  • Check the key accuracy heatmap. Your history view includes a per-key heatmap showing which keys you actually miss.
  • Use Weak Keys mode. It biases practice words toward your lowest-accuracy keys, so you drill the problem instead of re-practicing what you already do well.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is typing accuracy calculated?
Accuracy is the share of character positions you typed correctly on your first attempt. Every position in the passage is scored exactly once, on the first keystroke that reaches it — later corrections don't change the score for that position.
Do backspaces lower my accuracy?
No — and they can't raise it either. Deleting characters isn't counted at all, and retyping a position that was already scored adds nothing. The error you originally made stays in your error count.
What is a good typing accuracy?
Aim for 95% or better before chasing speed. Skilled typists usually hold 97–99%. Below 95%, the time you lose noticing and fixing errors typically outweighs any raw speed gain.
Why is my accuracy lower here than on other typing tests?
Many tests re-score a position after you correct it, which lets backspacing hide mistakes. This test scores each position once, on the first attempt, so the percentage reflects what your fingers actually did.
