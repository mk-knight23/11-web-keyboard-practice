How to Type Faster

Whether you're a beginner or pushing past a plateau, faster typing comes from the same three things: correct technique, accuracy-first practice, and honest measurement.

Start Practicing Now

The six fundamentals

1. Master the home row

Place your fingers on A-S-D-F (left hand) and J-K-L-; (right hand), thumbs on the space bar. This is the foundation of touch typing — every finger has designated keys to reach, so your hands stop drifting and your brain stops searching.

2. Focus on accuracy first

Speed without accuracy is counterproductive. Aim for 95%+ accuracy before pushing for higher WPM: every error costs the wrong keystroke, the backspace, and the retype. TypeSprint's scoring makes this impossible to fake — each character is scored on your first attempt, so corrections can't hide mistakes (the accuracy test page explains the model).

3. Practice consistently

Fifteen minutes of daily practice beats occasional hour-long sessions. Motor skills consolidate through frequent, spaced repetition. Use the timed mode for structured sessions — the week-by-week plan below gives you the progression.

4. Don't look at the keyboard

The hardest habit to break, and the most impactful. Cover your keyboard with a cloth if you must. Your muscle memory develops dramatically faster when your eyes stay on the screen, because the feedback loop runs through your fingers instead of your eyes.

5. Train your actual weaknesses

After a few tests, open your history: the key accuracy heatmap shows exactly which keys you miss. Weak Keys mode then biases practice words toward those keys — drilling the problem instead of re-practicing what you already do well.

6. Vary the material

Words drill common patterns; quotes add natural punctuation and capitalization; code mode forces symbols and brackets. Varied input prevents your speed from being an artifact of one text style.

Track what's actually improving

Every test you finish is saved to your local history with WPM, accuracy, and mode. The progress dashboard draws a trend line from your recent tests — improvement should show as a slope over weeks, not day-to-day jumps. If the line is flat, change the practice (different mode, weak-key drills), not the effort.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to type faster?
With 15 minutes of focused daily practice, most people see a measurable WPM gain within 4–8 weeks. Breaking a long-standing plateau takes longer than gaining early speed, because you're rebuilding habits rather than adding them.
Should I focus on speed or accuracy first?
Accuracy. Errors cost the wrong keystroke, the backspace, and the retype — plus the pause to notice. Hold 95%+ accuracy at a comfortable pace and speed rises on its own; chase speed first and you cement error habits.
How often should I practice typing?
Daily, in short sessions. Fifteen focused minutes every day builds motor memory far better than an occasional hour — typing skill consolidates through frequent repetition, not marathon sessions.
